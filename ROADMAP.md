# Roadmap

Notes for picking the project up in a fresh session, on any computer. The rules of the code base and how we work are in `CLAUDE.md`, and the finished features are documented in `README.md`. This file holds what neither of them does: where the library stands, what is planned, the design decisions behind the plans, and what was rejected.

## Where the library stands

Implemented, documented and reviewed. Every generator is a preset, a composition of public steps run by `pipeline` (see Composable generation and Underground generators on steps under the design decisions), and the steps of all of them mix in one map:

- **Composition**: `pipeline`, `step`, `emptyMap` and `MapState` with its `prunable` and `reserved` masks; areas (`area`, `count`, and `placement` for the cave steps); retries from a fresh base with `attempts` and `failures` in every result.
- **Mazes** (`generateMaze`: `placeMazeRooms`, `carveMaze`, `openMazeRooms`, `addExits`): recursive backtracker, bias, loops, patterns (whirlpool, snowflake), exits, ordinary rooms and templated rooms.
- **Dungeons**: room-first (`generateRoomDungeon`: `scatterRooms` or `partitionRooms`, then `carveCorridors`, with `connection: 'nearest' | 'bsp' | 'bsp-random'`) and grown (`generateDigDungeon`: `growRooms`, then `carveCorridors`), layout and corridor symmetry, room templates (rotations, mirroring, exits, doors). `carveCorridors` also joins any other floor on the map (caverns, halls, mines) to the rooms.
- **Caves** (`generateCave`: `growCave`, `digCaveTunnels`, `addExits`): cellular automaton, regions, tunnels between caverns with optional loops, exits, layout symmetry. `digCaveTunnels` also joins rooms through their potential exits.
- **Noise** (`createSimplexNoise`, `createFractalNoise`, `transformNoise`): public building blocks, no dependencies. It stays in this package; extracting it into a separate library was considered and rejected (the forest needs a default noise, and the code is small and isolated in `src/noise/`).
- **Noise caves** (`generateNoiseCave`: `carveNoiseCave`, `digCaveTunnels`, `addExits`): a cave from thresholded noise, with `shape: 'blobs' | 'veins'`, `fill` as a quantile and `cornerBias`.
- **Forests** (`generateForest`: `plantTrees`, `addStreams`, `addRoads`, `placeClearings`, `placeTemplates`, `addClearingPaths`, `addFootbridges`): noise-driven tree density, roads with crossings and branches, exact roads from a `specs` list, a verge (`clearance`, `falloff`, `distance`) on every feature, `Tile.Grass`, `Tile.Tree`, `Tile.Road`, and clearings (radii are `Range`s), with optional paths to them and clearings at the dead ends of branches (`at: 'branch-end'`).
- **Streams and rivers** (`addStreams`, `addFootbridges`): a stream is a road made of water, laid before the roads, with shallow banks and a deep core, tributaries, springs, bridges where roads cross (generated roads are planned across them and laid again through the plan, crossing at `bridgeAngle`), footbridges that join the land a walker cannot wade, a verge beside the water, and `at: 'stream'` clearings. Roads and streams end cleanly at the border. See Streams and rivers and Roads across streams under the design decisions.
- **Mines** (`generateMine`: one of `digFishbone`, `digVeins`, `digPillarHalls` and `digRings`, then caverns of `carveNoiseCave` joined by `digCaveTunnels`, `placeMineRooms`, `addExits`): four layouts, noise caverns, rooms and room templates with doors, and exits. See Mines under the design decisions.
- **Templates in forests** (`placeTemplates`, and `clearing`, `chance` and `margin` on `RoomTemplate`), and the retry rule for templates everywhere. See Templates in forests under the design decisions.

The tile values are part of the public contract and never change: `Wall` 0, `Floor` 1, `Door` 2, `Grass` 3, `Tree` 4, `Road` 5, `ShallowWater` 6, `DeepWater` 7, `Bridge` 8.

The source layout and its import rule (imports only point down: `core`, then `pipeline`, then `rooms` and `exits`, `noise`, `roads` and `streams`, then the generators, which may also use `cave/` and `maze/` as listed) are in `CLAUDE.md`.

## Design decisions worth keeping

- **Forest `clearance` and `falloff` stay separate.** The first version of `clearance` thinned the tree chance gradually, and the user wanted a clear zone. So `clearance` is a hard strip, and `falloff` is a separate soft ramp beyond it.
- **Clearings are placed, never found in the density map**, so one is guaranteed. Plain discs were rejected, the user wants irregular shapes (domain warping with a flood fill). Radii are approximate, since the ragged edge varies the real size by up to about 40%. A clearing that finds no room is dropped, with no relaxing, since there is no minimum count (a minimum count would be the case for relaxing).
- **Clearings at branch ends are never dropped for overlapping.** Two that merge look natural, and several paths may lead to one glade. They take the branch ends in the order the branches were laid, and come last in the `clearings` result.
- **Exact roads (`roads.specs`) replace the generated network** rather than adding to it, and placement is best effort: geometry is clamped or cut short, and only a bad parent index and similar mistakes throw.
- **Mines are one preset with four layout steps** (`digFishbone`, `digVeins`, `digPillarHalls`, `digRings`), one key each on `generateMine`, at most one given (fishbone by default). They were first one generator with a `layout` option, not a mode of the digger and not four generators, since the layouts share the carving core, widths, density, caverns, rooms and exits; the move onto steps made each layout a step of its own (one step per variant). Two layouts in one map are two step calls, each in an area. The code is in `src/mine/` and builds on `src/roads/` (centerlines, brushes), `src/noise/` and the cave steps.
  - **Shape of the code.** `carvePassage` and `tracePassage` carve and trace a winding passage of a width, between two tiles or from a tile at an angle. A layout lays the tunnels (`lay-fishbone`, `lay-veins`, `lay-pillars`, `lay-rings`, each wrapped by its step), and the preset then adds caverns (`carveNoiseCave` and `digCaveTunnels`), rooms (`lay-rooms`, wrapped by `placeMineRooms`) and exits (`addExits`). `MinePassage.kind` says what a tunnel is: `shaft`, `adit`, `vein`, `ring`, `spoke` or `tunnel`.
  - **`density`** is the target share of floor, best effort: it stops short without throwing when there is no room. The defaults are 0.15, 0.35 for `pillars` (its halls need room to show pillars) and 0.3 for `rings` (it only picks the ring count). For pillars it only sizes the halls, and the veins between them follow `hallVeins`.
  - **fishbone**: a straight main shaft (windiness 0.1, `angle` random if omitted, width 2 to 3) through a point near the middle, and adits (width 1 to 2, windiness 0.4, length 15 to 50) off random tiles of any tunnel at 45 to 90 degrees. An adit stops short of other tunnels with a wall between, so it ends in a dead end. `loops` is the chance that it breaks through instead. Things that were hard to get right: adits ignore their parent only until they pull clear of it, a dead end must touch the mine in one place only, and a shaft that winds off the map is cut to the stretch around its origin.
  - **veins**: junctions on a jittered grid (`veinSpacing`), the shortest spanning tree always dug, then extra links between near neighbors, shortest first with some shuffling, until `density`. So the web-ness is set by `density`, and `loops` is fishbone only.
  - **pillars**: a few halls (`halls`, `hallSize`, `hallRagged`, 4 tiles of rock apart, shrinking to fit when the size comes from `density`) joined by `hallVeins` veins that leave the boundary of a hall and are routed through the rock around the halls (see Pillar hall veins below), which grow into a network that may end veins on veins. Pillars (`pillarSize`, `pillarSpacing`, `pillarRagged`, `pillarJitter`) stand on a lattice centered in a hall, and each needs floor all round it and never covers a vein. Loops of veins can shut in rock of any size, which is not a pillar.
  - **Pillar hall veins: a network that grows, with a detour limit.** It replaced two earlier versions that the playground rejected: a noisy pinned line between random tiles of two halls (veins ran middle to middle, erased pillars and overlapped), and then veins routed around the halls between planned pairs, which sometimes ran around a whole hall and half of another, since the random entrances landed on sides that could only be joined by a long way round and nothing bounded a route.
    - **The network grows from a first vein** (`grow-hall-network.ts`): the nearest pair of halls is joined (the next nearest if that fails), then the hall nearest the network, with distances recomputed after each, by one vein to the nearest boundary tile of any hall or vein in the network. A vein may end on another vein (a T-junction) but never crosses one, which would make the wall-between-veins rule much harder. Every hall ends up with one vein out, so the mine is connected by construction, with no union-find and no removal of veins.
    - **Extras**: `hallVeins` is the total, so the extras are `hallVeins` minus the veins above, at least 0. Each starts at a random hall and ends on the nearest hall or vein that does not leave that hall, so it makes a loop and not a stub. With no other target, or no route, the extra is left out (joining it to the hall's own vein would make no difference to a mine that is already connected). Ends within 3 tiles of an entrance or end already used are avoided where possible.
    - **Entrances** (`find-hall-boundary.ts`, `pick-entrance.ts`): boundary tiles, never interior ones. `hallEntrances` is `'facing' | 'edge'`, default `'facing'`; `'any'` was removed, since it is `'facing'` that also destroys pillars and the library is unpublished. The start is picked from the band of boundary tiles about 3 deep on the side toward the nearest network tile (or the whole boundary), 10 tiles tried, the one furthest from the junctions so far kept. The end is the nearest network tile to the start. A vein's boundary is that of the floor it carved, found with `findHallBoundary`.
    - **Routing** (`dig-routed-vein.ts`, `find-rock-route.ts`, `wobble-route.ts`): an A* on the grid (edge steps only, with a tiny pull toward the straight line so that equal routes do not run in an L) between the rock tiles next to the two ends. Blocked are the map border, all floor, and everything within the vein's reach plus one tile of floor (`floor(width / 2) + 1`), so a wall is always left between a vein and a hall or an earlier vein. Close to its own ends the clearance is lifted, otherwise a vein could not leave or arrive. The wobble smooths the corners and pushes the route sideways by noise, up to 3 tiles at `veinWindiness` 1, shrinking the push where it would hit a blocked tile and falling back to the plain route when anything still does.
    - **Detour limit**: a route of more than `1.5 × distance + 8` steps is rejected, the distance being Manhattan between the two ends, since routes step along edges and a perfect diagonal is up to 1.41 times the straight line. The search stops early. Constants (`DETOUR_FACTOR`, `DETOUR_SLACK`), to be tuned in the playground; no option. Best-of-several routes was considered and left for later.
    - **Retries**: 10 attempts per vein between other starts and a freshly picked width; with `'facing'` the first 5 keep to the facing side and the rest use any side, since two halls that meet at a corner leave one channel between them, and the vein that takes it blocks the others.
    - **No hall is dropped for want of a vein**: a hall that finds no route is tried again once the others are in, then with the rules relaxed (only floor blocks, no limit, veins may run close), which squeezes through a one-tile gap, so a boxed-in hall joins what it is boxed in by. The old removal of a hall that no vein reaches stays in `layPillars` as a guard that should never be reached (its lines are the only uncovered ones).
    - **Rejected**: entrance tiles that stay inside the hall's outer band (the vein then cuts into the hall); keeping `'any'` as the "old look"; falling back to the straight pinned line when there is no route (it would cross halls and each other); a planned tree between hall middles (the greedy nearest-first order replaced it).
    - **Known limit**: halls only 4 tiles apart take one vein at a time through the gap, and wide veins (3) need the whole gap; a crowded map therefore gets fewer extras than `hallVeins` says.
  - **rings**: concentric ovals fitted to the map (`rings`, `ringWidth`, `ringWobble`), or circles with `ringShape: 'circle'`, closed loops that wobble by smooth noise, joined by at least one radial spoke per pair of rings (`spokes`). Ovals by default, so that a wide map is filled. `ringShape: 'circle'` sets both radii to the smaller one, centered, so the ends of a wide map stay rock; `density` keeps its meaning (the share of the whole map), so a circle on a very wide map may stop short of it. There is no common main shaft, since the spokes do that job.
  - **Caverns**: since the move onto steps, the preset's `caverns` are `carveNoiseCave` with `placement: 'beside'` (sides of 12 to 24, scales of 7, largest piece only, `count` 1), joined by `digCaveTunnels`. A cavern goes at the best of 100 random places where its floor and the tiles round it are free (the nearest to the mine); copies up to `count.min` that find none fail the map, the rest are left out. The old `lay-caverns` (20 places, joined by a short vein) is gone.
  - **Rooms and templates** (`placeMineRooms`, the preset's `rooms` key): `roomCount` (1 to 1) and the room sizes, `templates` (the shared `RoomTemplate`), `exitsPerRoom` (1 to 3) and `doorChance` (1), copied from the maze. In rock only, 2 tiles clear of the mine and of each other. An ordinary room that finds no spot or cannot be joined is left out, and a template that cannot be placed or joined throws a `TemplatePlacementError`. Every opening gets a winding tunnel to the nearest floor round any room in the way. No two openings of a room are next to each other, diagonals included. Ordinary rooms use their own spot search, since the shared template placement scan was too slow with the whole mine as one obstacle (15 seconds for ten rooms on 200 by 120); templates still use the shared code.
  - **The border**: a ring of wall always closes the map in, except for the exits. There is no `borderWalls` option.
- **Streams and rivers** (`src/streams/`, built on `src/roads/`). A river is a stream with a larger width range, one `streams` option.
  - **Tiles**: `ShallowWater` 6 ("passable at the cost of getting wet"), `DeepWater` 7 (needs swimming), `Bridge` 8. A game may read both waters as plain water. Playground glyphs: bridge `#` Yellow, shallow `≈` BrightBlue, deep `≈` Blue.
  - **Banks shallow, middle deep**: `shoreWidth` (default 1) tiles of shallow water on each side, and a deep core of `width - 2 * shoreWidth` along the centerline (a brush, not a distance from the bank, which gave deep tiles at the bends of a narrow stream). So widths 1 and 2 are all shallow and width 3 has a one-tile band.
  - **Laid before the roads** (a natural feature has priority), on a road map of its own. The water flows the way of `angle`, so `points` run upstream to mouth; tributaries (the branches of the stream, leaving against the flow at 30 to 60 degrees) are listed reversed, from source to confluence. `spring: true` grows the stream one way only.
  - **Road wins**: road tiles over water are `Bridge`. `bridges` in the result has a point per group of bridge tiles. How generated roads cross is under Roads across streams.
  - **Footbridges** (`lay-footbridges.ts`): only a stream 2 or more wide cuts the land (shallow, but it wets the clothes); one 1 wide is stepped through and never forces a footbridge, though the `bridges` minimum may put one on it. The large pieces of walkable land (roads and their bridges count as land, trees are passable; pieces under 20 tiles, `MIN_PIECE`, stay islands, such as the scraps cut off where a tributary and its stream leave the map side by side) are joined nearest first. A footbridge goes square across the stream from a centerline tile (skews of 10 and 20 degrees either way when square fails), walking out of the water both ways, laid as a staircase that touches along edges (the user chose this over a thin diagonal, so every crossing can be walked along edges), no longer than the stream's width plus 2, never on the edge of the map. Large pieces that no such footbridge joins stay apart, which never happened in 800 random layouts; the user is fine with that ("bridges are fairly rare anyway"). A fallback at any length and direction was built and dropped, since it makes exactly the long footbridges along a stream this replaced. `bridges` is a minimum count on top, spaced 8 tiles apart. A spring stream needs none.
  - **Banks and trees**: `bankClearance` and `bankFalloff` (default 0, trees often grow at the water's edge) have their own ramp, apart from `clearance` and `falloff`, sharing `distance`.
  - **Clearings on a stream**: `at: 'stream'` is centered on a random tile of any stream centerline, placed like a random clearing (spacing, inside the map), no path, water stays water.
  - **Left for later**: ponds, lakes and swamps centered on stream tiles (a stream that ends in a lake), fords, rivers in other generators.
- **Roads across streams** (`src/streams/`; rewritten in one pass of six tasks after the user found artifacts in about a third of the seeds: sharp turns where the old steering patched only the 6 tiles nearest the water, roads doubling back where it merged stretches, roads and bridges along the bank where its gap from the water fell back to 1 tile, footbridges along a stream, square road ends).
  - **Plan, then lay.** A generated road (main road, crossings, branches) is grown as before. One whose middle never comes into its verge is left alone; any other is planned (`plan-crossings.ts`, on the `StreamTerrain` of `create-stream-terrain.ts`) and laid again through the plan (`create-steer-across-streams.ts`). The reshape hook of `addRoad` is told the road's width. `roads.specs` roads and paths to clearings are not planned.
  - **The verge**: the tiles a road's middle keeps off, the water grown by `gap = floor(width / 2) + 1`: a square core of `gap − 1` (so a road's brush never touches water) and a round rim of `gap + 0.5` (so a path round it bends smoothly; a square verge made 90° bends at its corners). No fallback to a smaller gap.
  - **Ends**: an end on the border in the verge slides along the border to clear land (`clear-road-end.ts`: same side and the land of the nearest clear grown tile first, 30 tiles). An end inside the map (a branch's start) keeps the grown road up to its first clear tile (`head`, `tail` of the plan). An end on the border may also slide up to 30 tiles along its side when that makes its leg to the first (or from the last) crossing gentler (cost 1 per 30 tiles, plus 1 when the leg leaves the border at less than 30°). So `angle` is best effort near streams; the user accepted roads whose angle changes a lot.
  - **Routes**: two are planned and the cheaper kept: the pieces of land the grown road visits in order (scraps under 20 tiles skipped, loops cut, gaps filled by the fewest hops), and the fewest hops outright, which crosses a stream once below a confluence instead of the tributary and then the stream, with its crossings no farther than 20 tiles from the grown road. Each alone was worse: the grown route forced pairs of close crossings at incompatible angles, the fewest hops sent roads along the edge to far crossings.
  - **Crossing sites**: every centerline tile of every stream, four directions at the `bridgeAngle` drawn once per crossing. The line walks out of the verge both ways and must go from the right piece to the right one; its water may be no longer than `width × √2 / sin(angle) + 2` and its dry verge no longer than two banks need (`2 × gap × √2 / sin(angle) + 2`), so a bridge never runs along or beside a stream; straight leads of 4, 2 or 0 tiles clear of the verge, never touching the border. The road must reach the approach from the waypoint before (and the end from the last departure) over clear land within `1.5 × Manhattan + 8` steps. Sites are ranked first by distance from the grown road (within 10 tiles, then 20, then any), then by a score: the sharper leg's turn rate (twice the larger heading change at either end against the chord, over its length, in degrees per tile) over 3, plus drift and detour over 10, plus 0.5 near a confluence, plus 3 for every extra band of water. The best 5 per crossing are tried depth first, backtracking when one leaves no way on; a leg sharper than 6° per tile is rejected unless no plan avoids one.
  - **Laying**: a leg between waypoints is a Hermite curve (`curve-tiles.ts`, with a sideways `sway` and a `tension`) pushed sideways by the road map's noise at the road's windiness, `windiness × min(12, length / 4) × sin(πt)`, like `growPinnedLine`, on a lane of its own per road, so the bends fade out at every waypoint and every bridge is met square on. A free heading at an end mirrors the other end's about the chord (an even arc), or follows the chord. Tried in turn, 2 headings × tensions 1, 0.6, 1.5 × windiness 1, 0.5, 0; the first wins that keeps off the verge, never comes back within the road's width (after `2 × width + 1` tiles), never runs 6 tiles within a tile of the border, strays from the chord by no more than a quarter of its length plus 3, and turns no more than 45° by `sharpestTurn` (`src/roads/sharpest-turn.ts`); else the first that keeps all but the turn. Otherwise the land path (`find-land-path.ts`, A* with diagonal steps of √2 that never cut a blocked corner, laid as steps along edges, so it keeps near the straight line; a breadth-first search took any of the equally long ways along a row and then a column, often the edge of the map), kept off the outer two rows of the map but near its ends where it can, pulled taut (`smooth-path.ts`) and rounded by a Catmull-Rom spline (`spline-tiles.ts`) through every 8th, 5th or 3rd tile when that keeps the rules. The planner checked that the land path exists for every leg. A road with no crossing is patched stretch by stretch where it entered the verge, unless that turns over 45° or doubles back, when it is laid as one leg from end to end.
  - **Bridges**: a plan carries the road's width, and the forest makes bridge of the brush along every straight run (approach to departure) over water. `findSpans` stays as the backstop for the roads that are not planned: a group of road tiles over water that joins no two pieces of land gives way to the water.
  - **Brush past the border** (`src/roads/extend-past-border.ts`, used by `stampRoad` and `findDeepWater`): every road, stream and path whose end lies on the border goes on until it is 2 tiles out, along its heading (6 tiles back), a tile at a time along its faster axis, every extra centerline tile off the map, so a road at a shallow angle tapers along the edge. One along the border goes straight out, one under about 14° leaves at that angle. A width of 1 gains nothing. `points` and `exits` stay on the map.
  - **The checker** (`npm run check:forest`, `scripts/check-forest.ts`, unpublished; the user wants it in the repo for later fixes): the playground's forest at 80 by 50 with Road and Stream checked and `tributaries` 2 (`--tributaries`, `--seeds`, `--from`, `--to`, `--show`), seeds 1 to 200, counting: a turn over 45° by `sharpestTurn` (every tile averaged with 2 each side, the heading over 4 tiles each way; unsmoothed, the stairs of a path along edges made 35° over 6 tiles fail 184 of 200 grown roads; calibrated so that grown roads turn 39° at most and every seed the user flagged 48° or more), doubling back, running along the edge (12 tiles within 1 of the border) and along water (4 centerline tiles on land with the brush on water), road tiles given back to water, long footbridges, square road and stream ends, with the grown roads as a baseline. Since task 9 it runs `createForestSteps` with `pipeline` and takes the roads as grown from `trace('addRoads', ...)`; the playground's "Show grown roads" and "Show crossing plans" do the same.
  - **Counts** (200 seeds, tributaries 2, before → after): turn over 45° 88 → 11, doubles back 15 → 1, along the edge not measured before → 2 (grown roads 2), along water 11 → 2, given back to water 19 → 2, long footbridge 4 → 0, square road end 55 → 0, square stream end 55 → 0, footbridges 539 → 65. With no tributaries: along water 1, everything else 0. The 11 sharp turns left are mostly 46 to 53°, two crossings close together at a confluence.
- **Templates in forests** (`src/forest/`: `place-forest-templates.ts`, `assert-forest-templates.ts`; the retry is `pipeline`'s). Built in three tasks, reviewed.
  - **Retry, then throw.** A template that finds no place regenerates the whole map, up to 3 maps (`MAP_ATTEMPTS`, no option), and then throws the last `TemplatePlacementError`, since the input is then wrong and not the map unlucky. The reason: a boss arena or a scripted ruin cannot silently vanish. `TemplatePlacementError#permanent` marks errors that no other map could fix (the template is bigger than the map, no variant fits the lattice or is allowed), thrown at once. It was first built as wrappers round the old bodies of `generateMine`, `generateRoomDungeon` and `generateDigDungeon` (`retryPlacement`); since the move onto steps it is the 3 attempts of `pipeline` for every generator, mazes included, and `carveCorridors` routes up to 3 times inside its step before it fails the map.
  - **`clearing` and `chance` are fields of the shared `RoomTemplate`**, ignored outside forests, and so is `margin`. `chance` is per copy, not per clearing, so that `count` keeps its meaning (offering a template to every clearing with a chance would make five boss arenas on five clearings). A copy that is rolled in goes on a random free clearing of the pool where it fits, one template per clearing, centered; a template with `clearing: true` that finds none gets a clearing of its own at a random spot; any other template goes to a random spot and leaves the trees alone. `positions` and `areas` throw in a forest.
  - **Own clearing** is the box of the template, free of trees, and not an oval of `clearings`; the `margin` is a tree ramp beyond the box, like `falloff` (`away / (margin + 1)`), measured with `distance`, so there is no bare rectangle. `ForestTemplate#ownClearing` says it. A pool clearing is never grown.
  - **Tiles**: `Floor` and `Wall` force `Tile.Floor` and `Tile.Wall`; `Unused` leaves the map. Exit, door and optional exit cells throw a `RangeError` (`validateTemplate` cannot be used: it demands an exit).
  - **Roads and water win, and the template is placed after them, not before.** The signed-off design was to place the template first and route roads and streams round it. That was not built: roads and streams have no obstacle support, so the template is placed after the streams and the roads (and after the clearings), on a spot where none of its non-`Unused` cells lies on a road, a path or water, which is checked before the paths to clearings are laid. The path to the clearing of a template ends on the tile before the first template cell on the straight line from its start (`stopAt` of `ClearingPath`). A path to another clearing that still runs through a template, a rare case, makes the forest fail, which triggers the retry. In 200 random layouts (roads, a stream with tributaries, three clearings with paths, two templates) none failed. Routing roads round a template stays possible later.
  - **Left for later**: the "road connection" cell that joins a road to a template, templates in other generators' clearings, caves.
- **BSP corridors** are derived from room positions (a median split along the most spread axis), not from the real partition tree, so they work with every placement, templates and symmetry.
- **Underground generators on steps** (the mine, cave, maze and dungeon, moved onto steps in 21 tasks after the forest; reviewed). Seeds give other maps than before the move, as the steps draw in another order.
  - **The `reserved` mask.** `MapState.reserved` is a byte per tile next to `prunable`, with the values of `Reserve` (part of the contract, like `Tile`): `None` 0 (free), `Solid` 1 (never dug through) and `Openable` 2 (never dug through, but a step may open it as an exit). Room steps reserve their footprint, walls included, since a room wall is `Tile.Wall` just like rock. `reserveTile` sets it, `openReserved` marks an opened exit (the tile and every `Openable` tile round it, diagonals included, become `Solid`, so no two exits end up side by side), `isFree` is "holds the base tile or is prunable, and is not reserved", and `findNearestOpenable` is a breadth-first search to the nearest potential exit. The forest's `placeTemplates` reserves its cells, so an underground step cannot dig through a ruin.
  - **Room shapes.** Every room, ordinary or templated, has a shape in the dense `roomShapes` extra (a `TemplatePlacement` plus its door decisions): an ordinary room's is a ring of `OptionalExit` round its floor (`createRoomShape`). That gives one code path for openings; `findRoomOpenings` and the builder's rectangle branches went. Room steps reserve from the shape (`Wall` and `Floor` as `Solid`, potential exits as `Openable`), so steps that know nothing of rooms can find and open their exits. Whether a room is templated is still told by `Room.template`, never by having a shape: shared-wall doors, fixed exits, dropping and `fill` key on it.
  - **Symmetry is per step.** Every step that can be symmetric takes its own options: `symmetry` and `symmetryChance` for the mirror layout everywhere, `corridorSymmetry` and `corridorSymmetryChance` for corridors, `pattern` and `patternChance` for the maze. A symmetric map passes the same settings to each step, and partial symmetry (symmetric rooms with free corridors) is allowed. With an area, a symmetry mirrors about the area's center.
  - **One step per variant, never a mode switch**: four mine layout steps, two cave steps, three room placement steps (`scatterRooms`, `partitionRooms`, `growRooms`, on the shared `roomPlacementStep`). A caller who wants a random variant writes a chooser step. Corridor linking stays one step (`carveCorridors` keeps `connection`), since it only picks which links to build. One exit step, `addExits` (in `src/exits/`), serves every generator with border exits; the maze uses it with `lattice: true`.
  - **Areas and counts.** Feature and room steps take an `area` (`AreaSize`: `{ width?: Range; height?: Range }`, the whole map by default, never larger) and most a `count` (`Range`, default 1, may be 0 for an optional feature). A smaller area goes on a random free box with a margin of one free tile (`placeAreas`), and the step builds on a scratch map of the box (`buildInArea`) and shifts its extras by the offset; a generic wrapper was rejected, since it cannot shift arbitrary extras. A box that finds no spot is an ordinary error, which the pipeline retries, with a message that says what to do. Steps that join or open what is there (`digCaveTunnels`, `carveCorridors`, `openMazeRooms`, `placeMineRooms`, `addExits`) work on the whole map.
  - **Cave placement.** The cave steps take `placement`: `'free'` (the default, the rule above), `'beside'` (the cave is built and culled first, then put at the best of 100 spots where its floor and the tiles round it are free, nearest to the dug ground; the old mine caverns' rule, needed because a free box was rarely found next to a mine) and `'overlap'` (any box clear of the other copies, floor laid on free tiles only, a cave-in). The cave steps cull their own regions (`minRegionSize`, `largestOnly`) on their scratch map; a `cullCaves` step was rejected, since culling the whole map would eat other features.
  - **Halls and caverns are features, not rooms.** They come from their own steps in an area, never appear in `rooms`, reserve nothing, and `Room` has no `kind`. Joining them is the corridor step's job: `carveCorridors` treats every piece of floor outside the rooms' shapes as a stand-in room (`findStandIns`), whose free walls are its potential exits. Corridors route round it; it is never dropped (one cut off fails the map), gets no shared-wall door and no `Exit` entry, and distances to it are measured to its nearest wall, since a mine's box covers the map. `digCaveTunnels` joins every piece of floor whatever made it, and enters rooms that nothing reaches through their nearest `Openable` tile.
  - **Dungeon retries.** Only templated rooms and stand-ins can make a dungeon fail; an ordinary room that cannot be joined is dropped with its twins (its tiles back to rock and unreserved). `carveCorridors` routes on a copy of the map up to 3 times (10 at first: over 1,200 maps the 4th to 10th routings saved none, and a failed one cost up to 0.7 s), then throws a `TemplatePlacementError`, and the pipeline builds a new map. Two reachability floods (`canReach`) let hopeless corridor searches give up early without changing any map: one before the builder's last resort, and one between a stand-in and a room.
  - **The digger is gone**, replaced by `growRooms` plus `carveCorridors` (`generateDigDungeon` kept its name). Most of the digger's look came from where it put rooms, so `growRooms` grows each room off the wall of another at a `gap` (default 1 to 4, the user's pick at the comparison; 0 is a shared wall, which becomes one door). Lost and accepted: rooms off a fork in a corridor (`branchChance`), the exact parent-to-child tree and the random-walk corridors. Grown rooms cluster like the digger's (spread 0.57 against 0.58 of the map), but the pieces of corridor are longer (17.6 tiles against 10.0), since routed corridors share exits and merge where each dug one was a piece of its own.
  - **Presets.** Each generator is a composition, listed by an internal `createXSteps` and run by `pipeline` with 3 attempts; options are nested, one key per step, and results gain `attempts` and `failures`. No setting given to one key reaches another step (no config inheritance, the user's rule after task 17). `generateMine` takes at most one layout key (two layouts are two step calls in a pipeline of your own), `generateCave` computes `regions` after the pipeline (regions go stale, so they are no extra), and `generateRoomDungeon` takes `scatter` or `partition`, never both. Templates alone need `roomCount: { min: 0, max: 0 }`, since every room step places one ordinary room by default.
  - **Hidden state is rebuilt, never passed privately**: from the extras, or from the grid and the `reserved` mask. `pipeline/` sits right under `core/`, so every family may use it.
  - **Figures** are measured by `npm run check:dungeon` (`scripts/check-dungeon.ts`): failures, time and look of the room-first and grown dungeons, and rooms round a pillar hall and a noise cavern, over 200 seeds. No dungeon failed at the defaults; at 15 and 20 templated rooms a handful of seeds needed a second map.
  - **Known limits**: rooms for a maze in a smaller area cannot be made to land in that maze's area (two steps pick their spots on their own; see Rules for area spots in the backlog); a room whose wall touches a feature directly cannot open straight into it, since a corridor needs a free tile between; a mine of rings fills the map, so `'beside'` caverns fail on most such maps unless `count.min` is 0.

### Composable generation (steps)

Done: the forest in ten tasks, recorded below, and the underground generators after it (see Underground generators on steps under the design decisions). Instead of one generator with a bajillion options, a map is composed from an empty base map and a list of steps, like middleware. The forest is the pilot.

```ts
pipeline(
  emptyMap({ width: 80, height: 50, tile: Tile.Grass }),
  [
    plantTrees({ scaleX: 40, density: { min: 0, max: 0.25 } }),
    addStreams({ ...streams, clearance: 1, falloff: 3 }),
    addRoads({ ...roads, bridgeAngle, clearance: 1, falloff: 2 }),
    placeClearings({ clearings, spacing: 2, falloff: 3 }),
    placeTemplates({ templates }),
    addClearingPaths(),
    addFootbridges({ minimum: 2 }),
  ],
  { random, attempts: 3 },
);
```

**Decisions**

- **The old contract is broken on purpose.** Angstloch has never been released, so existing options may change or go. `CLAUDE.md` says so.
- **The base is an empty map**: `emptyMap({ width, height, tile })`, nothing else. Grass for a forest, wall for a dungeon or mine. The forest's `groundTile` option goes away, since it is the base tile. The base is a factory, so every attempt starts from a fresh state.
- **State and steps.** The state is `{ grid, base, prunable, ...extras }`. A step is `(state, ctx) => extras`: it mutates `grid` and `prunable` in place (safe, since a retry rebuilds the state) and returns only the extras it adds (`streams`, `roads`, `clearings`...), which the pipeline merges into the state. `ctx` holds the shared `Random`. Called by hand, a step takes an optional context that defaults to `resolveRandom`. Plain steps with a pipeline loop were chosen over a `next()` chain: the pipeline knows which step threw (for the failure record and the retry), nothing needs to act after the later steps, and the chain types stay simple.
- **Types express dependencies.** A step is `Step<Needs, Adds>`, and `pipeline` chains them, so `addClearingPaths` before `placeClearings` is a compile error. Optional use (roads cross streams only when `streams` exists) is a `Partial` need. For plain-JS callers a missing extra throws a clear error naming the step that is missing. "Streams before roads" is documented, and checked at run time where it matters. The chain type may give unreadable errors; if so, fall back to overloads or a `.then()` builder (prototype in the first task).
- **Ground cover first, features after.** `plantTrees` plants only on tiles that still hold the base tile. A step that runs before it gets no verge.
- **Prunable is a mask**, not "tiles equal to the tree tile": the state carries a byte per tile. `plantTrees` sets it and any step that stamps a tile clears it, so a template's wall cells are never pruned by mistake. A tile is free to build on if it holds the base tile or is prunable (this replaces the `covered` and `water` masks for templates and clearings). In a dungeon nothing is prunable, so a verge prunes nothing.
- **Every feature step prunes for itself** with a shared `Verge` option (`clearance`, `falloff`, `distance`, all defaulting as today): within the clearance the chance of pruning a tree is 100%, across the falloff it declines. A clearing is a ragged shape that is 100% pruned inside, with the ramp beyond it, as today. Verges from several steps combine, so overlapping ones prune slightly more than today's single ramp; defaults get tuned in the playground. This replaces the shared `clearance`, `falloff`, `distance`, `bankClearance` and `bankFalloff` of `generateForest`.
- **Option mapping.** `emptyMap`: `width`, `height`, `tile`. `plantTrees`: the noise options, density range, the tile to plant (it need not be a tree) and `density` as an extra. `addStreams`: the water part of `StreamOptions` and the water tiles. `addRoads`: `RoadOptions`, `roadTile`, `bridgeTile` and `bridgeAngle` (it describes how roads cross). `placeClearings`: `clearings`, `spacing`. `placeTemplates`: `templates`. `addClearingPaths`: the `path` part of the clearings (after the templates, since a path stops at its template's edge). `addFootbridges`: an explicit opt-in step, with the `bridges` minimum; a stream through a cave gets none unless asked.
- **Hidden state** is rebuilt from public extras. The stream terrain of the crossing planner is rebuilt from `streams` (centerlines and widths) in each step that needs it, at the price of one extra pass.
- **The composition API is public** (steps, `pipeline`, `emptyMap`). Calling steps by hand works. Presets stay: `generateForest`, `generateMine` and the rest remain as ready-made compositions built on the public steps, and any number of presets can exist. **No config inheritance** (user, after task 17 of the underground port): settings that enter a step never leave it. A preset wires each key straight to its step, with that step's defaults, and fills no option of one step from another key; the caller gives each step what it needs. So `generateRoomDungeon`'s corridors take no `placement` or `symmetry` from the rooms, `generateMaze` gives `rooms` no `keepClear` from `maze.start` and `exits` no `lattice: true`, and `generateForest`'s paths to clearings have a `paths` key of their own instead of the tiles and verge of `roads`. A `steps` option on the generators is left for later, as sugar.
- **Retry belongs to the pipeline**: up to 3 attempts by default, always, re-running from the base when any step throws. The result reports `attempts` and `failures`, one entry per failed attempt (`attempt`, step name, error), also when a later attempt succeeds. After the last attempt the last error is thrown. A `TemplatePlacementError` with `permanent` set skips the retries. This replaces the `MAP_ATTEMPTS` wrappers (`build-*.ts`).
- **Validation when a step is created**: `addRoads(options)` throws a `RangeError` at once, before any attempt, so only a throw while the map is built is retried. A step is a named function (`step('addRoads', fn)`), which gives the failure record its names.
- **Diagnostics.** `pipeline` takes an `onStep(name, state)` callback, so the playground can show the map after each step. A step's internal data (grown roads, crossing plans) goes through an optional `ctx.trace?.(name, data)`, which replaces `ForestObserver`.
- **The playground is rebuilt on steps**, as part of the work.
- **Decisions of the forest's planning session**: `generateForest` options become nested per step (`{ width, height, random, trees, streams, roads, clearings, templates, footbridges }`, each key holding that step's options, with the verges inside each feature); there is no `density` anywhere in the API (the chance of a tree is internal to `plantTrees`, the verges never see it, and a reporting option can be added later); the scope is the forest only, and mines and dungeons kept `retryPlacement` until they moved onto steps too, which removed it.
- **Built in task 1** (`src/pipeline/`): `step(name, { needs, adds }, run)` takes the extras by name as well as in the types, since `pipeline` checks the order up front at run time from the declared names; `pipeline` has a `trace` option as well as `onStep`, which is what hands `ctx.trace` to the steps; it recognizes a permanent error by its `permanent` field (`pipeline/` imports only `core/`, not `TemplatePlacementError`); and the chain types are `S & ValidChain<MapState, S>`, which gave a readable error (`StepOrderError<"clearings">` on the step that comes too early), so overloads and a `.then()` builder were not needed. The results keep `base` and `prunable`, so that a caller may chain a second pipeline by hand.
- **Built in task 2**: `Verge` and `assertVerge` (`src/roads/verge.ts`), `pruneVerge(state, mask, verge, random)` (`src/roads/prune-verge.ts`) and the `plantTrees` step (`src/forest/plant-trees.ts`, options in `plant-trees-options.ts`). `pruneVerge` measures with `roadDistance` and keeps a prunable tile with chance `clamp((distance - clearance) / (falloff + 1), 0, 1)`; it draws one number only for tiles inside the falloff, so tiles in the clearance or beyond cost no draw. `density` is a `Range` whose ends are fractions (0 to 1), and `plantTrees` seeds its built-in noise from the context's `random`, then draws one number per base tile. Its options are checked when the step is made, by building the noise once with a stub source. `pruneVerge`, `Verge` and `plantTrees` are public, so steps of your own can use them. The steps page now runs `plantTrees` and a border step that prunes its verge.
- **Built in task 3** (`src/streams/`): `addStreams` (`add-streams.ts`, `add-streams-options.ts`) wraps `layStreams`, stamps the water (`shallowTile`, `deepTile`) and prunes the bank verge, adding `streams`. Its options are the water part of `StreamOptions` (`Omit<StreamOptions, 'bridges' | 'bridgeAngle'>`) plus the three fields of `Verge` flat on the options object, not under a `verge` key, as in the roadmap's example. The verge is only pruned when `clearance + falloff` is above 0, which saves the distance pass for the default. With `roads` already in the state it throws a `TypeError` marked `permanent`, so the pipeline does not retry a mistake of order. `rebuildStreamMap(streams, width, height)` (`rebuild-stream-map.ts`, internal) re-reverses the tributaries, stamps every stream with `stampRoad` and returns `Omit<LaidStreams, 'deep'>` with `water`, `map.roads`, `junctions` and `owner` equal to the originals (tested over five seeds). Its map has a flat noise, since the steering, the terrain and the footbridges never read it. `createSteerAcrossStreams`, `createStreamTerrain` and `layFootbridges` still take `LaidStreams` and so do not accept it yet; task 4 narrows their parameter to what they read (`map`, `streams`, `water`).
- **Built in task 4** (`src/streams/`): `addRoads` (`add-roads.ts`, `add-roads-options.ts`) is `RoadOptions` plus `roadTile`, `bridgeTile`, `bridgeAngle` and the flat `Verge` fields. It rebuilds the streams from the `streams` extra (`rebuildStreamMap`), builds a road map of its own, lays the network with the steering of `createSteerAcrossStreams` as `reshape`, and stamps `roadTile`, or `bridgeTile` where the crossing plans (`traceTiles` from approach to departure, brushed) or `findSpans` say so; any other road over water leaves the water as it is. It adds `roads`, `junctions`, `exits` and `bridges` (`findBridges` over the stamped road surface, so footbridges are `addFootbridges`' to add later). `ctx.trace('addRoads', { grown, plans })` replaces `ForestObserver`, and the wrapper that collects the grown roads is only installed when there is a `trace` or a steering. `StreamLayout` (`stream-layout.ts`: `map`, `streams`, `water`) is the parameter type of `createSteerAcrossStreams`, `createStreamTerrain` and `layFootbridges`, and `LaidStreams` extends it with `deep`. `CrossingPlan` and `PlannedCrossing` are public types now, for `trace`. **Checker**: a temporary copy of `scripts/check-forest.ts` run on `addStreams` + `addRoads` (the playground forest, 200 seeds, 2 tributaries) gave turn over 45° 7 (today 11), doubles back 1 (1), along the edge 3 (2; the grown roads have 1), along water 1 (2), given back to water 0 (2), square road end 1 (0, seed 187: the end of the road at the top of the map, cut by the stream), square stream end 0 (0), long footbridge 0 (0). A seed gives a different map in the two (the order of `random()` calls differs), so the counts are comparable only in size; the real checker moves onto `pipeline` in task 9.
- **Built in task 5**: `placeClearings({ clearings, spacing, falloff, distance })` (`src/forest/place-clearings.ts`) wraps `layClearings` and `pruneVerge`. The verge has no `clearance`, since a clearing is bare inside (the mask is pruned with clearance 0, so inside is certain and the ramp starts at the edge, as `away / (falloff + 1)` did). It reads `roads` (distances and branch ends, through `rebuildRoadMap` in `src/roads/rebuild-road-map.ts`, which stamps the reported roads on a new map whose noise is made from the context's `random`) and `streams` (the centerline tiles for `at: 'stream'`), and adds `clearings: PlacedClearing[]`, where a clearing that asked for a path carries the resolved `path: { width, windiness }` and one on a stream never does. Creation validates every spec with `assertClearing` against an unbounded map; the check that a center lies on the map is the step's `validate` (see below). `makePermanent` (`src/pipeline/make-permanent.ts`) is used by `addStreams` for its order error. Paths are laid by `addClearingPaths` (task 7), which finds its clearings by position in the extra.
- **Built in task 6**: `placeTemplates({ templates, distance })` (`src/forest/place-templates.ts`) wraps `placeForestTemplates` and `pruneVerge`. The free mask is `grid === base || prunable`, which replaces the road and water mask (`covered`, `water`) of `buildForestOnce`, so any tile that a step stamped, of ours or the user's, is blocked. The options have only `distance` of the verge, since the width of the ramp is each template's own `margin`; the margin of an own clearing is `pruneVerge` over the box with a falloff of `margin` and no clearance, the same ramp as before. `Floor` and `Wall` cells go through `stampTile`, `Unused` cells leave the map. It reads `clearings` (optional, by position, so `ForestTemplate#clearing` is an index into that extra) and adds `templates: ForestTemplate[]`. A template bigger than the map is now refused in the step's `validate` (`assert-template-fits.ts`, shared with `placeForestTemplates`), as a permanent `TemplatePlacementError` before the first attempt; a copy that finds no spot throws the ordinary one, which `pipeline` retries. The check that no path runs through a template belongs to `addClearingPaths` (task 7), which reads `templates`. The old `generateForest` is untouched until task 9.
- **Built in task 7**: `addClearingPaths({ roadTile, bridgeTile, clearance, falloff, distance })` (`src/forest/add-clearing-paths.ts`) wraps `layClearingPaths`. It needs `clearings` and reads `roads` (through `rebuildRoadMap`, so a path joins the road at a junction), `templates` and `streams` (through `rebuildStreamMap`, for the water mask), and adds `roads`, `junctions`, `exits` and `bridges` again: the rebuilt map's roads with the paths appended, which replace those of `addRoads` through the merge. A path stops at the first tile of its own template's box that is not free (not the base tile and not prunable), which is the `stopAt` of the old code, found from the grid instead of the template's cells. The throw for a path that runs through a template is on the new path tiles only, inside any template's box, not free and not water, so a road that was already on an `Unused` cell is no error; it is an ordinary `TemplatePlacementError` that `pipeline` retries. Like the old code, the brush of a wide path that stops at a template may still touch its walls and throw. A path tile over water is `bridgeTile` where `findSpans` says it spans the water, and stays water otherwise; `bridges` is recomputed with `findBridges` over the road surface (road tiles on land, bridge tiles over water), so `bridgeTile` must be the same as in `addRoads`. The verge is pruned from the new path tiles only. The old `generateForest` is untouched until task 9.
- **Built in task 8**: `addFootbridges({ minimum, bridgeTile })` (`src/streams/add-footbridges.ts`) wraps `layFootbridges`. It needs `streams` (rebuilt with `rebuildStreamMap`) and adds `bridges` again. It does not read `roads` at all, unlike the plan's table: the only land that matters is on water tiles, and there a road tile that stayed a road is a water tile holding `bridgeTile` in the grid, so the "crossed" mask is `water && grid === bridgeTile` and no road map is rebuilt. As a result `bridgeTile` must be that of `addRoads` and `addClearingPaths`, or a bridge of a road is not recognized as land and gets a needless footbridge. It stamps the footbridge tiles with `stampTile` and recomputes `bridges` with `findBridges` over the footbridges and the bridges of roads. The mask for `layFootbridges` and `findBridges` is the same, since `layFootbridges` takes roads only as `crossed`. `minimum` counts all bridges, as `StreamOptions#bridges` did.
- **Built in task 9**: `generateForest` (`src/forest/generate-forest.ts`) is `pipeline(emptyMap({ width, height, tile: Tile.Grass }), createForestSteps(options), { random })`, and `createForestSteps` (`create-forest-steps.ts`, internal, not in `index.ts`) lists the steps so that the playground and the checker can run them with a `trace`. `ForestOptions` is `BaseOptions` plus `ForestStepOptions`: `trees`, `streams`, `roads`, `clearings`, `templates` and `footbridges`, each holding the options of its step (so `clearings: { clearings: [...], spacing, falloff }` and `templates: { templates: [...] }`). Steps whose key is missing are left out, except `plantTrees`. Decisions made while building it, to confirm: **(a)** `addClearingPaths` runs when any clearing asks for a path, at first with the `roadTile`, `bridgeTile` and verge of `roads`; since the no-inheritance rule it has a `paths` key of its own; **(b)** `addFootbridges` runs by default whenever there are `streams`, as the old forest did, and `footbridges: false` turns it off (the step itself is opt-in); **(c)** there is no `groundTile`: the ground is `Tile.Grass`, and another base takes a pipeline of your own; **(d)** the result has `attempts` and `failures` and no `density`, and its `clearings` are `PlacedClearing[]` (with the `path` they asked for). Deleted: `build-forest-once.ts`, `build-forest.ts`, `forest-observer.ts`, `is-template-cell.ts`, and the three old flat-option test files, whose assertions were ported to the steps' tests in tasks 2 to 8 and replaced by `generate-forest.test.ts`. **Checker** (`npm run check:forest` on the preset, 200 seeds, 2 tributaries; the order of `random()` calls differs, so only the size of the counts compares): turn over 45° 9 (was 11), doubles back 1 (1), along the edge 1 (2; the grown roads have 4), along water 1 (2), given back to water 0 (2), long footbridge 0 (0), square road end 0 (0), square stream end 0 (0), footbridges 56 (65). No regression. The playground forest page runs the steps through `createForestSteps` for now (its density display mode is gone); task 10 decides its future.
- **Built in task 10**: the playground has no forest page any more. `demo/steps.html` is the one page for forests, and it is a step builder: the user adds any of the seven forest steps, moves and removes them, and configures each in its own card (`demo/step-defs.ts` lists the steps with their controls, `demo/step-fields.ts` has the field helpers and `Values`, `demo/steps.ts` the page). It runs `pipeline` on every change, shows the map after any step (`onStep`), draws overlays from the extras of that step's snapshot and from `trace`, and starts from a forest. The `border` and `flaky` scaffolding is gone; retries stay visible through `placeTemplates`. The old forest page's density display is gone for good. **The user's direction** (task 10 talk): angstloch moves fully onto the pipeline approach with all generators, and ideally the playground has no subpages, only available steps that are added and configured separately. So the other pages (maze, dungeon, cave, mine, noise) are due to fold into the steps page as their generators become steps. Not carried over from the old forest page: the clearing rows (replaced by counts and an exact JSON list), the plain tile mode (floor and wall for trees, water and bridges; the planted tile and the base tile can still be picked), and per-step tile pickers other than the planted tile.
- **`validate` hook on a step** (added after task 5, when the user asked why a step could not know the map size): `step(name, { validate })` takes a function of the base state, `MapStep#validate` carries it, and `pipeline` calls it once on a fresh `base()` before attempt 1, after the order check. Whatever it throws is thrown at once, with no failure record and no retry, so a bad option that depends on the map (a clearing's center outside it, later a template bigger than it) is a plain error and needs no `permanent` mark.
- **Naming**: `generateX` makes a map from nothing, and steps use verbs (`plantTrees`, `addStreams`, `addRoads`, `placeClearings`, `placeTemplates`, `addClearingPaths`, `addFootbridges`).
- **Ruins** become a step that decays walls and scatters rubble, usable on any map. Rooms in caves and mixed mine layouts are compositions of the existing steps now, and dungeons and mines carve into a wall map, as planned.

**Known difficulties**

- The order changes the map: all steps draw from one `Random` in order, so a seed gives the same map only for the same pipeline.
- Steps over a finished grid treat everything as an obstacle, which was too slow for the mine rooms (see Rooms and templates in Mines), so spot searches need care.
- Layout symmetry acts while a layout is being made, so each step that can be symmetric takes its own symmetry options (see Underground generators on steps).
- Footbridges count roads as land, so `addFootbridges` runs after every step that lays roads.
- A path to a clearing stops at the edge of its template, hence `addClearingPaths` after `placeTemplates`.

**Open**

- A `steps` option on the generators, as sugar for a pipeline of their steps with additions, is left for later.
- **Rules for area spots per feature.** A smaller area goes on a random free spot today. Possible rules, as additions: keep off roads and streams (as clearings do), stay near the existing floor (as the old mine caverns did, now `placement: 'beside'` of the cave steps only), or a pinned position, which would also let maze rooms land in the area of a maze that does not fill the map.

### Caves

- **Rooms and templates inside caves** are a composition now: `growCave` or `carveNoiseCave` in an area, a room placement step, then `digCaveTunnels` (which enters the rooms) or `carveCorridors` (which joins the cave as a stand-in). A preset key for it on `generateCave` is not wanted yet.
- **Overlap as a share**: `placement: 'overlap'` of the cave steps may become a fraction or a `Range` of a cave's tiles that may overlap other things (default 0 to 0), still never over reserved tiles.
- **Water and lava**: needs new `Tile` values and a rule that keeps lakes from cutting the map in two. Much later.

### Forests and roads

All of this is optional follow-up to the finished forest.

- **Glades and pools**, now that the streams exist (see Ponds, lakes and swamps; a pool in a clearing or at a stream tile was the design in mind, and found glades were to be dropped), depending on the clearing code: natural openings in the forest, such as a drained lake bed or a pond, made from the clearing shapes. Pools need a water tile (see Water and lava) and a rule that keeps them from cutting the map in two. A found-glade mode (detecting the openings that the noise already leaves) belongs here. Needs a design talk.
- **Forest connectivity and regions.** Left out on purpose until the user has seen what forests come out. If wanted, the cave's region and tunnel code would move into a shared directory, with `connect` defaulting to `'none'`, since forests are usually see-through.
- **To check: tributaries that overlap the main stream.** The user saw a tributary run over the main stream and make a loop, after the move to steps. It is thought to predate the pipeline (not checked), so it likely sits in how `layStreams` places and grows tributaries (`src/streams/lay-streams.ts`). Not wanted, though not a big issue; the user would rather tributaries never did that. Whether it can also happen between two tributaries is unchecked. Not for the session it was reported in; it needs a look and a design talk first.
- **Areas for the forest steps** (`plantTrees`, `addStreams`, `addRoads`, `placeClearings`, `placeTemplates`), as the underground feature steps have.
- **Roads in other generators**: the `src/roads/` module (`layRoads`) is meant to be reused by the village and the town. `RoadNetwork` is currently internal; export it when a generator needs it.

### Village, town and castle

- **Village or hamlet** is definitely wanted: a handful of buildings along a road or around a green, no walls. Roads from `src/roads/` and room templates for buildings are the likely ingredients.
- **Town**: streets, lots, buildings, an outer wall with gates (gates as `exits`), districts and plazas. A street grid with jitter, a radial layout and recursive subdivision of blocks into lots were considered; subdivision is the simplest.
- **Castle or fortress**: probably a variant of the town machinery (concentric walls, towers, gatehouse, keep, courtyards). Decide when designing the town.
- The tile model for buildings needs a decision before building (`Road` exists; building interiors, walls and floors may reuse `Wall` and `Floor` or get new values).

### Mines, follow-ups

The first entry is wanted; the rest are optional:

- **Roughly parallel adits in the fishbone.** The user wants the adits of `digFishbone` (and of the fishbone layout of `generateMine`) to run roughly parallel to each other, instead of leaving their tunnels at independent random angles as they do now. Needs a design talk.
- **Symmetry** for mines was deferred; it would fit the cave's layout symmetry.
- **Cave-ins**: parts of a mine cut off by a collapse, which the always-connected rule rules out now.
- **Mixed layouts**, for example pillar halls joined by a fishbone: two layout steps, each in an area of its own, then `digCaveTunnels`, in a pipeline of your own. The preset takes one layout only, and no array of layouts is planned.

### More generators

Wanted, not designed yet. Each needs a design talk. Prison and sewers are needed by the user's own game, so they come first.

- **Prison** (needed for the user's game): cell blocks along corridors in a rigid layout, a guard room, perhaps a yard. Related to catacombs (a structured dungeon, see below), so design the two together.
- **Sewers** (needed for the user's game): straight canals with walkways beside them, junction chambers and outfalls at the border. Likely built on the stream code (`ShallowWater`, `DeepWater`, centerlines, brushes, bridges) with rooms like those of `growRooms`.
- **Graveyard**: grave plots on a lattice, paths from `src/roads/`, a fence or wall, crypts and chapels from room templates, trees from the forest noise.
- **Ruins**: a decayed dungeon, town or building. Whether it is a generator of its own or an option on the existing ones is open (see the open questions below).
- **Farmland or countryside**: a patchwork of fields split by hedges and lanes, with farmhouses from templates. The natural surround of the village.
- **Mountains, ravines and canyons**: noise ridges and cliffs with passes and trails. Needs a cliff or rock tile.

Decisions:

- **New terrain tiles are decided when a generator needs them**, not in one batch up front. Add new values, never renumber.
- **Analysis helpers** (distance maps, farthest tile, dead ends, connectivity checks), should they ever be written, stay private, inside `src/`, and are not exported from `src/index.ts`.

Open questions:

- **Ruins**: a "post-processor" would be a function or option that takes a finished map and changes it, such as breaking gaps in the walls with noise and scattering rubble or trees, instead of generating one from scratch. It would break the rule that results are composed by the caller, so the user is unsure about it. The alternative is a `ruined` option (with a decay amount) on the generators that make buildings.

### Dungeons, follow-ups

- **Corridor-first dungeon**: tunnels laid first (straight, lattice-friendly), rooms sprouted off them where they fit, with doors, and dead ends where none fit. It is kept only if it makes dungeons clearly distinct from `scatterRooms`, `partitionRooms` and `growRooms` with `carveCorridors`, and from a mine with rooms; otherwise it is dropped. Needs a design talk.

### Catacombs

Undecided. They feel like a very structured dungeon, something like `growRooms` and `carveCorridors` made rigid (often on a lattice, with niches along the walls and a few crypts from room templates), and may or may not get a generator of their own. The mine is done, so this can be designed now. The carving core and the room and cavern code of `src/mine/` may carry over.

### Ponds, lakes and swamps

Streams and rivers are done. What is left is standing water, which the stream code now allows: a pond or lake centered on a stream tile (the design already has `at: 'stream'`), possibly with the stream flowing through it or ending in it, and swamps. The earlier idea of pools in clearings (a smaller ragged oval clipped to keep a bank of ground) still fits. A pool must not cut the map in two, which `layFootbridges` already guards for streams. Fords are open. Needs a design talk.

### Mazes and rooms

The first iteration of rooms inside mazes is done. Still open, from earlier sessions: mazes inside dungeon rooms, cellular automata inside rooms, and a shared "region" abstraction that several generators could use.

## Rejected

- **Multi-level dungeons.** The game generates the individual levels and joins them.
- **Wilderness or overworld.** Usually a single level, and games often make it by hand, so it is not worth a generator here.
- **A separate noise package.** See above.
- **Cellular automata for forests.** Noise gives broad woods and glades, and a cellular automaton cannot.

## Loose ends

- The base generators are considered finished, and the README no longer marks any of them as work in progress. New features are added as optional fields, and existing options and results do not change.
- Reference material for ideas: the old attempt in `../angstloch-old` and rot.js 2.2.1 in `../ur/node_modules/rot-js`. Reimplement algorithms our own way, never copy code.
