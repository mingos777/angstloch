# `digVeins`

`digVeins(options?)` is a step that digs a network of winding veins through the rock between the pieces of floor on the map, and joins them all into one: rooms, halls, caverns and the veins of earlier steps. It is the mine's counterpart of [`digCorridors`](dig-corridors.md), which joins the same pieces with straight corridors. On a map with no floor it digs a web of its own (see [the veins layout](../generators/mine.md#the-veins-layout)), and for the halls of a pillar mine see [the pillars layout](../generators/mine.md#the-pillars-layout). Like `digFishbone` it needs a map that starts as wall.

- **Pieces.** A piece is a set of tiles that are not wall, joined along edges: a room, a hall, a cavern, a vein. The two pieces nearest each other are joined first, then the piece nearest the network to the nearest tile of anything in it, veins included. A piece with several parts joined already counts as one.
- **Rooms.** A vein enters a room through a potential exit (an `Openable` tile of the [reserved](../composing.md#mapstate) mask). The tile becomes floor and is opened with `openReserved`, so there is no gap in the wall. A room is entered only where a vein arrives, so a room with exits in all four walls opens one.
- **Never inside a piece.** The routing is the one under [the pillars layout](../generators/mine.md#the-pillars-layout): it may not use any floor and no reserved tile, so the pillars of a hall stand, and no vein runs through a room. A vein starts from a tile of a piece that has free rock beside it, never from one that only touches a pillar.
- **Density.** `density` is the share of the rock that was free when the step began that the veins dig out, counted from the veins that join the pieces on. With `0` nothing else is dug. Above that, junctions are scattered, each one nearest the network is joined first, until the share is reached. On a map with pieces, a junction is joined by two veins, the second to another part of the network than the first, so it is a bend on the way between two parts and no vein ends in rock; a junction that cannot get both is left out. On an empty map the web is a web of its own, and a junction gets one vein, so it may end in rock. When the junctions give out, finer ones are scattered in between (down to about 6 tiles apart), and then extra veins make loops. The share is a target, met as far as the map has room, so a crowded map stops short.
- **Thin walls and boxed-in pieces.** A vein keeps a wall between itself and the floor where it can. Where that leaves no way (a single wall between two caverns, as in a cave), a second try lets the vein pass close to the floor, and a last try lets it run through the rock that floor shuts in, which a piece surrounded by others needs. That last try is only made for a piece that nothing else reaches, so the pillars of a hall are safe whenever a hall can be joined at all. A piece that still cannot be reached is left as it is.
- **No slipping past a corner.** Where a dug vein touches other floor corner to corner only, with wall on both tiles between, a walker who moves diagonally could slip past the wall. A tile that the vein's width added there goes back to wall; a tile of its middle line gets one of the two tiles between dug, which turns the corner into an edge, unless both are reserved or at the edge of the map. A vein that cannot be mended is not dug, as if there were no route.
- **A piece that cannot be reached** (sealed in, or walled in by reserved tiles) is left as it is.
- **Cell blocks.** A [cell block](place-cell-blocks.md) is one piece, its cells and corridor joined by their doors, and its only potential exit is its entrance, so a vein enters it there, as it enters a room, and nowhere else.
- **Touching pieces.** An `Openable` tile with the floor of two different pieces on opposite sides, such as a block entrance that a later hall reaches, is opened as floor, before the veins are dug and again after, since no vein through rock can reach it. Once two pieces are joined that way, no other such tile between them is opened.
- **Uses** `passages`, `rooms`, `roomShapes` and `roomExits`, and **adds** `passages`, `rooms` and `roomExits` again: its own `vein` entries appended to `passages`, and every way into a room that it digs as an [`Exit`](../rooms.md#exit) appended to `roomExits` and listed in the room's `exits`: the `Openable` tile it opens into a room, and where a vein reaches the floor of a hall or a cavern (see [ways into halls and caverns](../rooms.md#ways-into-halls-and-caverns)). Without rooms, `rooms` and `roomExits` are empty.
- **Speed.** About 25 ms on an 80 by 50 map for the default density, 40 ms with two or three halls to join, and up to 200 ms for a `density` that asks for more than the map has room for.

```ts
const { grid } = pipeline(
  emptyMap({ width: 80, height: 50, tile: Tile.Wall }),
  [placeScatteredRooms(), digPillarHalls(), digVeins({ density: 0 })],
  { random: mulberry32(42) },
);
```

| Option           | Default    | Meaning                                                                                                                                              |
| ---------------- | ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `density`        | 0.15       | The share of the free rock that the veins dig out, from 0 to 1. The veins that join the pieces are dug whatever it says. With 0 nothing else is dug. |
| `veinWidth`      | `{ 1, 2 }` | Width of a vein in whole tiles. One is picked for every vein.                                                                                        |
| `veinWindiness`  | 0.5        | How much the veins wind, from 0 (straight) to 1.                                                                                                     |
| `veinSpacing`    | 16         | About how far apart the junctions of the web lie, in tiles, a whole number of at least 3.                                                            |
| `hallEntrances`  | `'facing'` | Where a vein starts on a piece: `'facing'` along the side that faces the nearest part of the network, `'edge'` on any side.                          |
| `symmetry`       | `'none'`   | The symmetry that veins follow by getting twins: `'horizontal'`, `'vertical'`, `'both'` or `'rotational'`.                                           |
| `symmetryChance` | 1          | How often a vein gets its twins, from 0 to 1. Applies with a `symmetry` only.                                                                        |

**Symmetry.** With a `symmetry`, every vein gets twins with the chance `symmetryChance`, rolled once for every vein: once it is dug, every tile it carved is dug at its mirror images about the center of the map too (the same transforms as the [dungeons](../generators/dungeon.md#layout-symmetry)), and so are the doors it opened, where their images are `Openable`. A twin tile that is not free rock a vein can reach (the border margin, a reserved tile, a pillar, but not rock that veins shut in) is left out, so a twin can be partial, but it never digs into a room or a hall. Twins are listed in `passages`, after their vein (after both veins of a junction). The pieces that the twin veins join are the images of the ones the vein joins, so a map whose pieces are symmetric gets a symmetric network. On a map with pieces, a vein gets a twin only at the images where the twin would join two things too: where each of its ends lands on floor, or on an `Openable` tile for a door. A junction gets the twins of its two veins together, so a piece without a twin leaves no dead-end twin vein, only a vein with no twin there. The junctions of the web (`density`) are not mirrored themselves, so a web grown on an empty map is only roughly symmetric. The twins count toward `density`.
