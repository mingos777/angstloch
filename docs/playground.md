# Playground

The playground is a web page for trying out the generators and the steps of Angstloch before writing any code:

- **Try a generator or a step.** Load a preset, such as a forest, a mine or a dungeon, or build a map from steps of your own choosing, in any order they allow.
- **See what an option does.** Every change makes the map again, so you can watch an option move the map from one setting to the next.
- **Check a composition.** Go through the seeds and watch the attempts and the failures in the stats line, to see whether a list of steps holds up over many maps or keeps failing.
- **Copy the code.** The **Code** panel under the map holds the code that makes the map with [`pipeline`](composing.md#pipeline), and **Copy code** puts it on the clipboard, ready to paste into a project. Only the seed is left out: pass a seeded `random` to make the same map again.

## Running it

The playground lives in `demo/` in the repository and is not published to npm, so it runs from a clone:

```
git clone https://github.com/mingos777/angstloch.git
cd angstloch
npm install
npm run dev
```

It then runs at http://localhost:5180.

## The pages

The index page links to the steps page, where forests, caves, mines, mazes and dungeons are made, and to the noise page. At the bottom of the steps page is the template editor: add, duplicate and remove [room templates](room-templates.md), set their count, rotations, mirroring, accuracy, positions and areas, and paint their cells by clicking and dragging with the chosen brush. Templates are kept in the browser's local storage and can be exported and imported as JSON, which is the same list of `RoomTemplate` objects that the `templates` option takes. Paste the exported JSON into the Templates field of a room card to use the templates.

All pages have a **Display** switch. `simple` paints plain colored squares. `roguelike` shows the map the way a roguelike would, as colored ASCII glyphs on a black background, in the colors of an ANSI terminal: wall `#` (#555555), floor `.` (#aaaaaa), door `+` (#aa5500), grass `.` (#00aa00), tree `T` (#55ff55), road `.` (#aa5500), bridge `#` (#aa5500), shallow water `≈` (#5555ff), deep water `≈` (#0000aa) and a floor with a potential exit `.` (#5555ff). Walls with only walls around them (diagonals included, the map edge counting as wall) are left blank.

The **Download PNG** button next to the switch saves the map as currently displayed (in either mode, at the chosen tile size) as `angstloch-steps.png` or `angstloch-noise.png`, handy for illustrating settings.

The noise page paints a `NoiseFunction` as gray squares, black for -1 and white for 1, with controls for the size, the seed, the fractal options and the transform: `scaleX`, `scaleY` and `rotation`. The fractal controls start at the defaults of `createFractalNoise`, and the scales start at 40, which is a size that shows a few features on the default map. The stats line shows the lowest, highest and mean value.

The steps page is where every map is made: a page with no fixed generator, but a list of steps that you add, order and configure one by one. The form at the top sets the size, the seed, the base tile and the number of attempts. **Add a step** picks any of [`plantTrees`](steps/plant-trees.md), [`layStreams`](steps/lay-streams.md), [`layRoads`](steps/lay-roads.md), [`placeClearings`](steps/place-clearings.md), [`placeTemplates`](steps/place-templates.md), [`layClearingPaths`](steps/lay-clearing-paths.md), [`layFootbridges`](steps/lay-footbridges.md), [`digFishbone`](steps/dig-fishbone.md), [`digVeins`](steps/dig-veins.md), [`digPillarHalls`](steps/dig-pillar-halls.md), [`digRings`](steps/dig-rings.md), [`placeMineRooms`](steps/place-mine-rooms.md), [`digCellularCave`](steps/dig-cellular-cave.md), [`digNoiseCave`](steps/dig-noise-cave.md), [`placeMazeRooms`](steps/place-maze-rooms.md), [`digMaze`](steps/dig-maze.md), [`openMazeRooms`](steps/open-maze-rooms.md), [`placeScatteredRooms`](steps/place-scattered-rooms.md), [`placePartitionedRooms`](steps/place-partitioned-rooms.md), [`placeClusteredRooms`](steps/place-clustered-rooms.md), [`placeCellBlocks`](steps/place-cell-blocks.md), [`digCorridors`](steps/dig-corridors.md) and [`openExits`](steps/open-exits.md) and appends a card with a control for each of its options (a blank angle means a random one, ranges are a min and a max, the road and clearing cards take exact roads and clearings as JSON, the `placeMineRooms`, `placeMazeRooms`, `placeScatteredRooms`, `placePartitionedRooms` and `placeClusteredRooms` cards take templates as JSON, as the template editor exports them). A card has buttons to move it up or down and to remove it, **Load a forest** starts from the steps of a forest, **Load a mine** sets the base tile to wall and starts from the step of [`generateMine`](generators/mine.md) with its defaults (`digFishbone`), **Load a mine with caverns** adds `digNoiseCave` with the defaults of the preset's [caverns](generators/mine.md#caverns) (`'beside'`, sides of 12 to 24, scales of 7, the largest cavern only) and `digVeins`, **Load a cave** and **Load a noise cave** set it to wall too and start from the steps of [`generateCave`](generators/cave.md) (`digCellularCave` and `digVeins`) and of [`generateNoiseCave`](generators/cave.md#noise-caves) (`digNoiseCave` and `digVeins`) with their defaults, **Load a maze** sets it to wall and starts from the step of [`generateMaze`](generators/maze.md) with its defaults (`digMaze`), **Load a maze with rooms** from `placeMazeRooms` (3 to 5 rooms), `digMaze`, `openMazeRooms` and `openExits` on the lattice, **Load a dungeon** sets it to wall and starts from `placeScatteredRooms` and `digCorridors` with their defaults, **Load a grown dungeon** sets it to wall and the size to 61 by 41 and starts from the steps of [`generateDigDungeon`](generators/dungeon.md#grown-dungeons) with their defaults (`placeClusteredRooms` and `digCorridors`), **Load a dungeon with a hall and a cavern** sets it to wall and 61 by 41 and starts from a pillar hall of 16 by 12, a noise cavern of 14 to 18 by 10 to 14 (largest piece only), `placeScatteredRooms` and `digCorridors`, which joins the hall and the cavern as [features](steps/dig-corridors.md#features), **Load a cave with rooms** from a `digCellularCave` cavern of 24 by 16, 4 to 6 rooms of `placeScatteredRooms` and `digVeins`, which enters the rooms through their potential exits, **Load a prison** sets it to wall and starts from 1 to 2 blocks of [`placeCellBlocks`](steps/place-cell-blocks.md) with a guard room, `placeScatteredRooms` and `digCorridors`, **Load a lattice prison** does the same on the lattice, every step with `placement: 'lattice'`, and sets the size to 61 by 41, and **Remove all** starts from nothing. Every change runs the pipeline again, so a step put in the wrong order shows the error of [`pipeline`](composing.md#pipeline) under the map, and a bad option shows its `RangeError`. **Show after step** picks the map after any step of the last attempt, starting with the base, through `onStep`. It shows the last step by default and keeps following it as steps are added, moved or removed, until you pick an earlier one; picking the last again makes it follow again. The overlays, drawn from what the steps reported up to the chosen step, are **Mark clearing centers**, **Outline templates**, **Outline halls** (the box of every room of kind `'pillarHall'`, in yellow), **Outline caverns** (the area of every `'cave'` group of `digCellularCave` and `digNoiseCave`, in cyan), **Mark tunnels** (a small square on the middle line of every passage of the mine steps, red for a main shaft and blue for the rest), **Outline rooms** (the floor of every room, cells included, but the halls and caverns, in green), **Outline cell blocks** (the footprint of every cell block, its tunnel and its guard room in magenta, and its entrance in yellow, on by default), **Show room gaps** (the openings of rooms that got no door, as a floor with a potential exit), **Mark maze start** (a green square on the `start` of `digMaze`), **Mark junctions**, **Show reserved** (the [reserved](composing.md#mapstate) tiles, red where `Solid` and cyan where `Openable`), **Mark road exits**, **Show grown roads** (a small magenta dot on every tile of a road as it was grown, before the streams bent it) and **Show crossing plans** (outlined squares on the planned ends of each road and on the waypoints of its crossings: cyan where the road turns onto and off the straight run, yellow at both banks), the last two fed by `trace`. **Show walking distances** writes the walk of [`measureDistances`](distances.md) from a picked place to the center of every other place the steps reported up to the chosen step: rooms, halls and caverns included, by their floor from [`createRoomMap`](rooms.md#createroommap), clearings by their centers, and exits. **Measure between** set to `rooms only` measures with [`measureRoomDistances`](distances.md#measureroomdistances) instead, between the rooms, halls and caverns alone. Click a place on the map to measure from it; it is outlined in white, the farthest place is written in red, the places at least 70% as far in yellow, and `×` marks a place with no way to it. **Distance metric** and **Distance costs** (JSON keyed by tile number, such as `{"7": 10}` to swim deep water, and `1e999` for `Infinity`) are its options, and the line above the map names the farthest places and how long the measuring took. Point at a room, a hall or a cavern on the map and the line right above the map says what it is, from [`createRoomMap`](rooms.md#createroommap): its kind, id, group, box, and how many exits and doors it has, and how many of them it shares with another room; its floor is tinted and its exits outlined in magenta. The stats line gives the attempts, the failures and how many roads, streams, bridges, clearings, templates, passages, rooms, room exits and room groups there are, the rooms counted by `kind`, and the regions of floor of the finished map when it has floor, and the failures are listed under the map; a template that finds no room is an easy way to see a retry. The planted tile of `plantTrees` and the base tile can be tree, wall, floor or grass, so a steppe of rocks on floor is a click away. In the `roguelike` display, grass is a green `.` and a tree a darker green `T`.
